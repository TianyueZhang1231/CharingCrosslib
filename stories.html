文化響應 · 雙語空間 · 研究方法

Why this library, why two languages, and how the identity was drawn from a single page.

A redesign project by -Tianyue Zhang, working between London and the Chinese-speaking world, made in response to the street the library stands on.

01我們的故事

A platform for
cultural exchange

At the heart of Charing Cross Library is a commitment to making Chinese-language knowledge more accessible within London's multicultural landscape. The library is conceived as a platform for cultural exchange, where Chinese and British communities can meet through reading, learning, and shared experiences.

Guided by values of diversity, equality, and openness, the library fosters a space where knowledge is not only preserved but actively shared. Its personality is intelligent yet approachable, rational yet warm — bridging cultures through thoughtful design and accessible information.

Through its services, programmes, and spatial design, Charing Cross Library aims to become a living cultural interface: a place where languages coexist, ideas circulate, and communities connect.

The site

Charing Cross Road, WC2H

Charing Cross Library stands in the centre of London, a street away from Chinatown. International visitors and people from many language backgrounds gather around it — and public libraries, as a rule, do not speak to them.

Research for the project began with observation: how people use the building, which languages are spoken at the desk, and how little of the collection announces itself to a reader who does not already know where to look. It continued with a review of the visual language of public libraries — and the discovery that they mostly share one: functional, institutional, and slightly cold.

This redesign answers that with three things designed together: language, brought into a single bilingual typographic system; cultural programming, planned as a seasonal rhythm rather than a list of one-off events; and spatial experience, in which the atmosphere of the room is treated as content.

Natural, sustainable materials replace the cold, hard surfaces of the institutional library: wood-fibre acoustic panels, woven textile signage, recycled paper and lime-washed plaster. The aim is a library that is culturally responsive — one where knowledge is not only read, but perceived through language, material and atmosphere.

02方法

Design method

Four moves, in order. The identity came first because everything else — the wayfinding, the shelf ends, the programme notes, the window film — is drawn from it.

01

Research

Site reading, conversations with library staff, and a survey of how public libraries in London present themselves in print and in space.

研究
02

Identity

A mark and a bilingual wordmark drawn from the ruled page, tested as a poster before it was tested on screen.

識別
03

Materials

Wood-fibre acoustic panel, woven textile, recycled paper, lime plaster — chosen to absorb sound, hold warmth and be repaired.

材料
04

Space

Shelving, lighting, seating and signage developed as one system, with Chinese and English shelves arranged by subject rather than by language.

空間

Process note, 02

Logo design

Knowledge is not contained, but flows in; not divided, but connected.

Inspired by Gitanjali 35, the design frames the library as an open and evolving field of knowledge. Rather than a fixed or hierarchical system, it proposes a space that is approachable, continuous and non-restrictive.

Each variation in the length of the ruled lines reflects the uneven expansion of thought. The misalignment avoids a fixed hierarchy, suggesting a non-linear, non-authoritative structure of knowledge. Slight variations introduce an intentional flow, referencing the idea of reason as a continuous stream rather than a static structure. The spacing between the lines creates a rhythm, echoing the pauses and intensities within reading and thinking.

03導視

Line and plane

The wayfinding system is developed from the same generative logic as the logo, based on the relationship between line and plane.

Lines function as directional elements, guiding movement through space, while planes define users and spatial anchors. The use of cutting is consistent across both — not to divide, but to reveal structure, allowing elements to remain connected and permeable.

This continuity ensures that the wayfinding is not applied as a separate layer, but grows directly from the identity, forming a coherent and open system.
